# Running the Adventure

## *Everyone Here Has Enough*

> *"Everyone here is trapped in their own version of 'enough.'"*

This is for the Dungeon Master. It contains a complete adventure — *Vaelithra IV: Gilded Folly* — a trial-gauntlet one-shot in which the party is imprisoned inside a luxury hotel that punishes its guests by giving them exactly what they think they want.

> ##### Adventure Information
>
> **Recommended Level:** 1 to start (each Trial auto-scales the active combatants to Level 5 / 8 / 10 / 12, and the finale scales the whole party to Level 15)
>
> **Party Size:** 4–6 characters
>
> **Play Time:** 8–10 hours
>
> **Timeline:** Any point in the Vaelithra Saga — the hotel is not fixed to one place or time
>
> **Setting:** The Gilded Folly Jail Hotel
>
> **Adventure Type:** Trial Gauntlet • Psychological Horror • Morality Play
>
> **Canon:** Vaelithra Saga IV

## Overview

*Gilded Folly* is a narrative-driven gauntlet: the party begins as prisoners inside a jail disguised as a luxury hotel, and over the course of the adventure is repeatedly, individually pulled into arena trials against champions who embody different faces of Greed. Unlike *Vaelithra V* and *VI*, this adventure is built around one boss fight after another — but every fight is also a lesson, teaching a specific mechanic (risk-and-reward, target priority, battlefield control, resource restraint) that the party needs again, amplified, in the final confrontation.

Underneath the Trials runs a second, silent game: two entities, **Avariel Mammeth** (Demon of Greed) and **Alethia Caelion** (Angel of Charity), are using the party to settle an old argument, and the DM is quietly tracking which side the party's choices lean toward. The party is never told this is happening. It should still shape how the hotel feels to be inside.

Here's an overview of what you'll find:

- **Running the Adventure.** This file: an overview of the hotel, the Greed vs. Charity system, the Trial structure, and guidance on running the adventure's tone.
- **Adventure Sites.** Five parts follow the party from arrival and their assigned rooms, through all four Trials, to Pride's uninvited intrusion and the final battle against Cael Verix.
- **Appendices.** Two appendices describe the magic items and the creatures that appear in the adventure.

## Adventure Background

After the Sundering War, **Vaeil**, God of Time, rewrote history to end a war neither side could truly win. To protect the Archive that made that act possible, he shattered his own soul into twelve fragments — the **Vaelithra** — and scattered them to be discovered, inhabited, or become something else entirely. Most fragments became people. One became a place.

That place is the **Gilded Folly Jail Hotel** — a prison where every inmate is given luxury, and their punishment is quietly tailored to their sin. Long before this adventure begins, the hotel was unknowingly purchased by **Avariel Mammeth**, a Demon of Greed looking for a profitable piece of real estate. He has no idea what he actually bought. He now runs it as three things at once: a place of punishment, a stage for divine trials, and a source of profit — never suspecting that the building itself is alive, watching, and slowly waking up.

### Adventure Outline

*Vaelithra IV: Gilded Folly* has five parts.

*Part I*, "The Gilded Cage," brings the party in as new prisoners, introduces the nervous receptionist **Vanka Yuriv**, and stages the first confrontation between Mammeth and Alethia over what the Trials are actually for — before each character is shown to a room that gives them everything they want and nothing they need.

*Part II*, "The First Trials," puts one player at a time into the arena: first a 1v1 against **Vauryx, Hoarder of Still Gold**, then — after an interlude that introduces the hotel's other trapped inmates and the party's first real look at Vanka's instability — a 1v2 against **Feltheris, the Golden Warden** and **Citrina, the Lustrous Siren**.

*Part III*, "The Weight of Ownership," escalates: the hotel's temporal glitches intensify, Vanka has a breakdown that hints she isn't what she appears to be, and two players face **Auric Vex, Arbiter of Dominion** in a fight built entirely around space and control.

*Part IV*, "The Mirror of Intent," is the last structured Trial — a 2v2 against **Aurelion, the Keeper of Excess** and **Seraphel, the Bearer of Burden**, reflections of the party's own Greed and Charity — followed immediately by Pride's uninvited arrival, which ends the Trial system for good and hands the party a power-up that is "not growth, but permission."

*Part V*, "Scion of Pride," is the finale: the full battle against **Cael Verix**, followed by Vanka's true nature finally coming to light, a keepsake for every player, and the hotel's abrupt, unexplained departure from the world.

### The Greed vs. Charity System

Every action the party takes inside Gilded Folly quietly nudges an invisible scale between **Greed** and **Charity**. This is tracked entirely by the DM — never explained to the players, never rolled for, never shown on-screen. Its only visible effect is atmospheric.

| Player Behavior | Points | Environmental Feedback |
|---|---|---|
| Choosing self-interest in a Trial trade-off, hoarding items, ignoring an NPC's suffering | **+1 Greed** | Gold surfaces glint harder and almost painfully bright; rooms feel warmer, stuffier; shadows stretch further than they should. |
| Sharing resources at a cost, helping an inmate, taking on risk for someone else | **+1 Charity** | Light softens; the hotel's ever-present clock-tick falls into sync; the air feels calmer, more natural. |

Don't tally this out loud or explain the mechanic if asked — let the players notice the hotel's mood shifting and wonder why. The scale has no hard thresholds or forced effects; it's a mood dial for you to lean on, not a system the party can optimize. It matters most as connective tissue across *Parts II* through *IV*, and pays off narratively as Vanka's instability grows: the hotel isn't just recording the party's choices, it's reacting to them.

### The Observer's Insight (Spectator Mechanic)

Since only one or two players fight in most Trials, give everyone else something to do. While a Trial is underway, the other party members watch through a *Major Image* projected into the lobby, and can act through it:

***Tactical Analysis (Action).*** A spectator makes a DC 15 Insight or Investigation check. On a success, reveal one trait or resistance of the current boss — for example: *"You notice the way Vauryx flinches whenever you spend a resource... his gold seems brittle there."*

***Emotional Support (Once per round).*** A spectator "shouts" through the image. Choose one: the active Champion gains advantage on their next attack roll, or the boss suffers disadvantage on its next attack.

***Resource Transfer.*** A spectator can throw a health potion or item into the image to help the active Champion — but Mammeth and Alethia both take a cut. On the **Greed path**, Mammeth "taxes" the transfer and the potion only heals for half. On the **Charity path**, Alethia allows it through untaxed, but the spectator takes 1d10 damage from the planar friction of forcing it across.

## Running This Adventure

Gilded Folly should feel wrong before it feels dangerous. For the first several chapters there's no overt threat — only a hotel that's too clean, too accommodating, and just slightly delayed in how it responds to the world. Let that unease build slowly; the horror here is atmospheric, not gory.

### DM Tips

**Champion selection should feel inevitable, not random.** Mechanically you're rolling to see who enters each Trial, but narratively frame it as the hotel choosing — "a pull," "a certainty," "you are being chosen." Never let it feel like the DM picked a target; let it feel like the building did.

**Use the boss's mechanical state to drive your flavor text, not the other way around.** Each Trial boss has a clear "stable" state and a clear "broken" state (Vauryx's Aurum Stasis, Feltheris's Perfect Alignment, Auric Vex's Dominion Field, Aurelion's Accumulation stacks) — narrate the shift the instant it triggers mechanically, so players learn the fight by watching it, not by reading a stat block. *Appendix B: Creatures* calls out the specific narration cue for each boss's break state.

**Vanka's arc is the adventure's real spine.** She should read as more unstable, more aware, and more sympathetic every time the party sees her — nervous receptionist, to breakdown, to partial reveal, to the truth in *Part V*. Don't rush her, and don't let Mammeth or Alethia upstage her scenes; she is the hotel.

**Mammeth and Alethia are judges, not enemies.** Neither one fights the party directly until the very end, and even then Mammeth is reacting to Pride's intrusion, not attacking the party out of malice. Play both with sincerity: Mammeth genuinely believes ownership and profit are just, Alethia genuinely believes the party's growth matters more than their comfort. Let their commentary between Trials feel like two very different people watching the same test and drawing different conclusions.

**Cael Verix is not testing the party's damage output — he's testing whether they can stop repeating themselves.** His core mechanics (Adaptive Dominance, Absolute Standard) punish predictable, single-strategy play. Make sure players understand through play, not exposition, that varying their approach and stacking multiple damage sources is what actually hurts him.

**Let the finale's music and pacing carry weight.** If you use table music, cut it the instant Pride steps through the rupture in *Part IV*, and don't bring it back until the party is fully re-leveled and ready to fight — let that silence land. When you frame the Level 15 power-up, say it plainly: this isn't a level-up, it's Vaelithra itself giving the party permission to become its Champions.

## Important NPCs

**Avariel Mammeth**, Demon of Greed, treats the entire Trial system like a high-stakes gambling match. He wants the party to survive — but only if they end up owing him. Once per session he can offer any player a **Greed Bargain**: a "Loan of Power" granting an instant critical hit or maximum damage on one attack or spell, in exchange for one of the player's most valuable items after the session (or a permanent -1 to a stat of Mammeth's choosing, representing interest). He bought this hotel as an investment property and has no idea he's actually the landlord of a living god-fragment.

**Alethia Caelion**, Angel of Charity, is a silent supporter of growth who believes in the strength of the group over the individual. Once per session she can offer any player a **Charity Sacrifice**: a "Shared Burden" letting a player automatically succeed on a saving throw, at the cost of two other players (of the target's choice) taking 4d10 unreducible radiant damage. She's watching to see whether the party can carry weight for each other, not whether they can win alone.

**Vanka Yuriv** appears to be nothing more than a nervous young receptionist — adjusting her glasses, avoiding eye contact, apologizing for things that aren't her fault. She is, in fact, the hotel itself: a **Human Vaelithra**-adjacent guardian bound to the Gilded Folly fragment, who has been quietly trying to help the party survive the Trials without breaking her own cover. Her composure cracks a little further every time the party sees her, until *Part V* finally reveals what — and who — she really is.

**Vauryx**, **Feltheris**, **Citrina**, **Auric Vex**, **Aurelion**, and **Seraphel** are the hotel's Trial champions — each a distinct expression of Greed (hoarding, control, dominance, excess) or, in Seraphel's case, its mirror in Charity. None of them are truly evil; each genuinely believes their trial teaches something true. Full stat blocks for all of them are in *Appendix B: Creatures*.

**Cael Verix, Scion of Xy'loth** is Pride given form — an entity from beyond the hotel's own rules, who tears his way in uninvited once the fourth Trial ends. He isn't interested in punishment, profit, or lessons; he's interested in proving that nothing here can match him. His arrival is the one moment in the adventure that isn't part of the hotel's design, and Mammeth's genuine fury at the intrusion should make that clear to the party.

**Fradonic Ra** and **Lunaris Thoht** are thrown through the rupture that heralds Cael Verix's arrival, flung out of a future battle at only a quarter of their strength. Both can lend the party a single, powerful one-time assist during the final fight (see *Part V*) before retreating to recover. Players who have run *Vaelithra V: The Sun God's Last Wish* will recognize Fradonic immediately — this is a deliberate cross-saga cameo, and Lunaris Thoht is the same name seeded as a hook at the end of that adventure. Neither NPC's presence needs to be explained in full; a glimpse of a larger, ongoing story is the point.
