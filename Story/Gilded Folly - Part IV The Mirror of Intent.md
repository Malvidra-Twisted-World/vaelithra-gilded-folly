# Part IV: The Mirror of Intent

> *"This is not growth. This is permission."*

The bell has rung four times, each ring overlapping the last, but not perfectly. Something is wrong. Something is watching.

## Trial IV: The Mirror of Intent

Two players are chosen for the final structured Trial, to face two champions who reflect their own choices back at them.

***Level Adjustment.*** The chosen players become **Level 12**.

> [!NOTE] <!--easygit-callout:original=info,collapse=--> Read Aloud
> Two of you feel it. Not a pull. Not pressure. Recognition. "You will be seen."

***The Fractured Reflection.*** A mirror-like floor, broken reflections scattered across every surface, floating shards of glass and gold, an environment that shifts subtly as it's watched.

> [!NOTE] <!--easygit-callout:original=info,collapse=--> Read Aloud
> The reflections move. Before you do. Two figures step forward — from your reflections. Not identical. Not copies. Distorted. Refined.

***Running the Combat.*** The two chosen players face **Aurelion, the Keeper of Excess** and **Seraphel, the Bearer of Burden** together (full stats in *Appendix B: Creatures*) — living embodiments of Greed and Charity respectively. Aurelion gains a stack (to a maximum of 5) every time a player uses a resource or deals burst damage; each stack grants him +1 AC and +1 to attack rolls, and at 5 stacks he gains an extra attack. If the players instead delay actions, coordinate low-resource play, and avoid burst damage, all his stacks reset. Seraphel, in turn, transfers half of any damage she takes to another target of the player's choosing — but if the players split up and focus her individually rather than staying together, she can't transfer damage at all, and takes an extra 2d6 on top. During Round 3, two shards appear to help manage both mechanics at once: **the Shard of Stillness**, which instantly zeroes Aurelion's stacks when crushed, and **the Prism of Isolation**, which disables Seraphel's damage-transfer for anyone fighting within 5 feet of it.

Aurelion rewards excess. Seraphel rewards unity. Together, they force the party to confront which one they actually are. Run the fight in three phases: confusion, as the players are unsure who to target and the mechanics aren't yet clear; recognition, as they realize Greed grows stronger with excess while Charity spreads damage between allies; and decision, as they consciously choose to control their damage output, position carefully, and break the bosses' synergy on purpose. Narrate the shift plainly when it matters: *"the more you give… the more he takes"* for Aurelion, *"he does not fall alone"* for Seraphel.

***Defeat.*** Both champions must be reduced to 0 HP.

> [!NOTE] <!--easygit-callout:original=info,collapse=--> Read Aloud
> The reflections crack. Not outward — inward. Both figures falter. "You… chose… …something…" They dissolve.

The hotel reforms afterward, but it no longer pretends to be ordinary — the walls breathe, the gold pulses, the space watches openly. Mammeth's tone has lost its amusement entirely: *"…enough."* Alethia, quietly: *"It is time."*

## Final Event: The Intrusion of Pride

This event triggers immediately after Trial IV concludes.

***Mammeth's Declaration.*** The arena stabilizes, perfectly, for the first time. Mammeth steps forward — no smile, no performance. *"It is done." "Greed prevails."*

***The Rupture.*** The sky above tears — not cleanly, not under anyone's control. Something forces its way through, and the gold around the party trembles, not in obedience, but in resistance.

***The Fall.*** Two figures are thrown from the tear, not descending but rejected, crashing hard into the arena. Power ripples unstably from both of them.

**Fradonic Ra** wears a jade-green haori and radiates intense sun energy, his body strained, breathing heavy. **Lunaris Thoht** wears luxurious red attire and emits controlled moon energy, his eyes sharp but weakened. Both arrive at roughly 25% HP, unable to fight further for now. If the party knows either name — Fradonic in particular, from *Vaelithra V: The Sun God's Last Wish* — let the recognition land without over-explaining it; this is a glimpse of a fight from somewhere else entirely. Optionally, let them exchange a line: Fradonic, *"…wrong place…"*; Lunaris, *"…no… wrong time…"*

***The Descent of Pride.*** The tear doesn't close — it widens. Something steps through, not thrown, not forced. Invited, by itself.

> [!NOTE] <!--easygit-callout:original=info,collapse=--> Read Aloud
> A humanoid figure, perfect posture, no wasted movement. Reality seems to align around him. Sound dulls. Space itself feels... inferior.

He looks over everyone present, then smiles slightly. *"A contest of strength?" "How quaint."* *"I will take all of you."*

***Mammeth's Reaction.*** This time, real anger. *"…you dare." "An inferior of Pride enters* my *domain?"*

***Alethia Steps Forward.*** Calm, but firm. *"This is no longer your domain alone."*

At this point Mammeth has no champions left to send, Pride refuses to follow the Trial structure at all, and Fradonic and Lunaris are too weakened to fight. Before the situation can escalate further, **Vanka** steps forward — no hesitation, no fear. *"Enough."*

***Vaelithra Awakening.*** The entire hotel hums — not violently, not chaotically, but intentionally. Time bends. The party feels something looking at them. Recognizing them.

***Power Bestowed.*** Every player is instantly and fully healed, every resource restored, and every character becomes **Level 15**. Make the framing explicit at the table: this is not growth. This is *permission* — Vaelithra itself choosing the party as its Champions, not a level-up they earned through XP.

The Pride Entity tilts his head, amused. *"Better." "Now… try."*

***Setting Up the Finale.*** The party now fights **the Pride Entity** — **Cael Verix, Scion of Xy'loth** — directly. Fradonic and Lunaris, though too weak to fight themselves, can each lend one powerful one-time assist during the battle (detailed in *Part V*). Mammeth watches, furious. Alethia watches, judging the outcome. This is not a Trial and not a test — it's an intrusion being rejected, and the tone should reflect that: higher stakes, less structure, no safety net.

## Ending the Chapter

Xy'loth's reach extends further than anyone in the hotel expected. Pride doesn't follow rules the way Greed and Charity do — and Vaelithra, for the first time in this adventure, has chosen to act rather than simply watch. Continue to *Part V: Scion of Pride*.
